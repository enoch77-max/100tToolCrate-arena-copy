About Readability Checker

Readability Checker: Make Your Writing Easier to Read

Paste an article, email, essay or landing page and this tool calculates six established readability formulas: Flesch Reading Ease, Flesch-Kincaid Grade, Gunning Fog, SMOG, Coleman-Liau and the Automated Readability Index, plus average sentence length, syllables per word and estimated reading time.

Readability scores are guides, not grades. A technical document for specialists can legitimately score as difficult, while a children’s story should usually score as very easy. Use the scores to spot long sentences, dense vocabulary and paragraphs that ask too much of the reader, then revise with your actual audience in mind.

How to Improve a Score

Shorten sentences, replace rare words with common alternatives, break long paragraphs and use active voice. Keep necessary technical terms but define them on first use. The tool works on English prose because the underlying formulas rely on English syllable and sentence patterns; results for other languages are not meaningful.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Flesch Reading Ease score?add

For general web content, 60 to 70 is a practical target. Scores of 80 or above are very easy, 50 to 59 is fairly difficult and below 30 is very difficult. The right target depends on the audience and subject matter.

Which readability formula should I use?add

Flesch Reading Ease is intuitive for general audiences, Flesch-Kincaid Grade maps roughly to US school grades, Gunning Fog highlights complex words, and SMOG is often used for health communication. Looking at several scores together is more useful than optimising one number.

Does the checker work for languages other than English?add

The formulas are designed for English. Syllable counting and sentence segmentation behave differently in other languages, so the scores would be unreliable. Use a language-specific readability tool instead.

Why do names and technical terms skew the result?add

The syllable counter uses a heuristic that does not know every proper noun or abbreviation. Long names, acronyms and hyphenated technical terms can inflate the syllable count. Treat the result as an estimate and review passages in context.

Quick tips

  • Paste directly into the editor - counts update as you type.
  • Use copy or download to move the result into your own workflow.
  • Clear the editor between unrelated documents to avoid mixing data.
