Editorial, Testing & Monetization Policy

This policy explains how 100ToolCrate handles accuracy, tool lifecycle, comparisons, authors, testing, monetization and corrections. It exists to make limitations visible rather than to imply certification.

What we test

The build validates every tool's registered handler, generated metadata, canonical URL, sitemap coverage and internal references. Backend tests exercise the real REST/MCP protocol, security boundaries, SQLite counters and deterministic execution functions. Production route tests check canonical URLs.

These checks do not prove that every file, browser, device, camera, PDF feature or third-party CDN response works. File-format support and practical size limits depend on the implementation and device. A page must not claim universal compatibility, lossless compression, guaranteed acceptance or “100% accuracy.”

Who writes and reviews

Existing blog bylines identify Max, site maintainer, based on the repository's authorship data; no academic, medical, legal, tax or other professional credential is implied. Generated layout and validation scripts assist publication, but a large number of pages is not treated as evidence of quality. We do not use fabricated reviewer names, credentials, testimonials, user counts or traffic figures.

Tool lifecycle

All 122 current tools have registered browser handlers and recorded activity in the legacy aggregate counters. That small dataset is not enough to declare low-use tools unwanted. Tools are retired for verified obsolescence, unfixable security risk, or replacement by a maintained equivalent—not simply to make the catalog look smaller. Homepage placement is editorial and usage-informed; the full directory remains searchable.

Comparison pages

The previous five competitor-alternative pages measured approximately 83–84% similarity and included unverified pricing, limit and superiority claims. They are marked noindex,follow, removed from the sitemap and unlinked from primary navigation until each can be rewritten with dated primary-source research. They are not presented as independent reviews.

Privacy distinctions

  • Free browser tools: supported file/text transformations run locally; third-party library files may still be downloaded from CDNs.
  • Public execution API/MCP: the selected text/data/calculation inputs are processed transiently on the server and are not persisted by that endpoint. Network infrastructure may keep standard logs.
  • Contact, service and sponsor forms: submissions go to FormSubmit for delivery to the site operator.
  • Paid service files: are not local-only. Transfer happens only after a written scope and approved channel are agreed.

Money and independence

Revenue options include consent-gated AdSense, labeled sponsorships, productized services and future paid products. Advertising does not buy followed links, tool rankings, editorial reviews or search rankings. Prices and scopes are published where practical; no invented conversion, revenue or client results are displayed.

Corrections

Report a broken tool or incorrect claim through the contact page. Include the tool URL, browser, input type (not the sensitive file itself), expected behavior and observed error. Security reports should omit secrets and use the security-disclosure subject.
