Price, calculate, and prepare a client invoice

Check the project margin, calculate applicable tax, prepare a clear invoice, and export a client-ready document without losing the working figures.

Time neededAbout 12 minutes
Useful forFreelancers, consultants, small agencies, and independent service providers
0 of 4 steps completeSaved only on this device
Open each tool in a new tab, then mark the step done when the output is checked.

An invoice should agree with the quote, explain the charges, and make payment unambiguous. Calculating tax or margin directly inside a final document makes avoidable arithmetic errors harder to spot.

Tax rules differ by jurisdiction and customer. Treat the calculations here as arithmetic aids, then confirm registration, rates, reverse-charge wording, and record-keeping duties with a qualified local adviser.

1

Check the project economics

Separate revenue from direct costs before interpreting margin. Include subcontractor fees and other delivery costs consistently; do not confuse markup percentage with profit margin.

  • Use the same currency for revenue and costs
  • Record assumptions in your project notes

2

Calculate the applicable tax line

Confirm whether displayed prices include tax before calculating. Keep the net amount, tax rate, tax amount, and gross total distinct so the invoice can be checked line by line.

  • Verify the legally applicable rate
  • Round according to your invoicing rules

3

Build the client-facing invoice

Add both parties' details, a unique invoice number, issue and due dates, itemized work, currency, and payment instructions. Match the scope language to the accepted quote or contract.

  • Use a sequential invoice numbering system
  • State late-payment terms only if agreed and lawful

Create a consistent document archive

If you maintain a text summary or supplementary note, convert the final version to PDF and store it with the invoice. Keep immutable issued copies separate from drafts for future periods.

  • Reconcile the exported total with your ledger
  • Back up issued invoices and payment status

Mistakes to avoid

Mixing up margin and markup

Margin uses revenue as the denominator; markup uses cost. A 50% markup does not produce a 50% profit margin.

Applying a tax rate without checking the supply

Customer location, tax registration, and the nature of the service can change treatment. Arithmetic alone cannot determine the correct legal rate.

Editing an issued invoice silently

Use your accounting process for corrections or credit notes and preserve a clear audit trail rather than replacing an already-issued number.

Frequently asked questions

Does this invoice workflow provide tax advice?add

No. It performs calculations and document preparation. You remain responsible for applicable law, registration, invoice wording, rates, and professional advice.

What makes an invoice easy to pay?add

A unique reference, clear due date, itemized charges, correct currency, accurate recipient details, and working payment instructions reduce avoidable back-and-forth.

Should I password-protect every invoice?add

Use encryption when the sensitivity and delivery arrangement justify it. Provide the password through a separate channel and ensure the client can actually open the document.
